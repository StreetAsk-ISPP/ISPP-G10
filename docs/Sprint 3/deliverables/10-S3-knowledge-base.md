## Feedback received

### 📅 09/04/2026 – Feedback

**Strengths**

- High-impact "Killer Opener" with carefully crafted, visual, and professional presentation aesthetics.
- Excellent user incentives and outstanding tone throughout the entire presentation.

**Weaknesses**

- Demo: Add dynamic text overlay on screen to clearly show changes during transitions.
- Team Building: Expand with concrete activities and include participation percentage metrics.
- Testing (Pyramid): Restructure the pyramid; integration should not be at the top, arrange tests in logical order.
- Testing (Concept): The integration testing slide is actually more End-to-End testing than integration testing.
- Clockify: Improve time tracking and logging accuracy.
- Storyboard: Better integrate it into the overall presentation narrative.
- Costs: Project cost estimation as pilot users grow and detail service-related expenses.
- Expenses: Remove decimal points from expense breakdown.
- Problem Metrics: Define specific thresholds or targets for each identified problem.
- Clockify Case: Formally present the Clockify incident as a problem, including solution and target threshold.

## General feedback from evaluation session

### 📅 09/04/2026 – General observations across groups

**Positive patterns observed**

- Present tense product messaging: Speaking about what the product currently is conveys maturity and credibility over using future tense.
- Complete Storyboard: Having differentiated versions for clients and investors is a highly valued strength.
- Real user verification: Confirming pilot user engagement through logs, screenshots, or direct contact instead of just listing names.
- Justified deployment diagrams: Explaining technical decisions behind provider and billing model choices.
- Custom QA strategies: Using well-explained, custom representations instead of generic AI-generated testing pyramids.

**Areas for improvement observed**

- Narrative disconnection: Inconsistencies between the killer opener, demo, and remaining sections; lack of solid narrative flow.
- Insufficient visual support: Scenes with black screens or videos where actions aren't visible due to lack of zoom or context.
- Linear cost graphs: Straight-line projections reduce realism in economic evolution modeling.
- Confusion in test types: Not clearly differentiating between integration testing and E2E testing.
- Omitted invisible costs: Failing to include free external services (GitHub, Gemini, MS 365) in the project cost breakdown.
- Disconnected economic forecasts: Estimating profits or ROI without basing them on real pilot user conversion and usage data.
- Illegible Gantt charts: Text too small or missing legends preventing understanding of actual project progress.

**For the next sprint**

- Ensure narrative coherence: Review transitions between sections to make the story flow smoothly from start to finish.
- Optimize demo visual resources: Add titles, zoom into key areas, and ensure no segments lack visibility.
- Refine performance metrics: Identify each team member by their initials and clearly explain data sources.
- Manage internal risks: Treat process non-compliance (e.g., not logging hours) as official risks with corresponding metrics and corrective measures.
- Deepen Team Building documentation: Record frequency, effectiveness, and actual participation, integrating it with team health metrics.
- Differentiate testing plans: Show concrete examples of what is tested at each level, avoiding excessive technical density.
- Detail Capex/Opex breakdown: Ensure all external services are listed and figures are rounded for easier analysis.
- Define pricing model: Establish plans with exact quantities and link them to profitability forecasts based on pilot users.
- Update methodology and accessibility: Reflect any workflow changes and include direct links to documentation.
